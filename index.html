Regional Team Maker

Form project teams around a shared geographic region of interest, with a balanced mix of skills: data analysis, slide design, library research, and public speaking. Each team should share a region and have at least one confident student for each part of the project.

Codes only. Upload student codes and survey answers, never names, usernames, or ID numbers. The tool runs in your browser and saves nothing. Keep the key workbook in IU-approved storage, as with any student record.

Step 1 Load survey responses

Export your survey (Qualtrics, Google Forms, or a Canvas survey) as a CSV with one row per student, then upload it or paste it below. A sample class is loaded so you can see how the tool works.

No student names or IDs. Do not upload names, IU usernames, email addresses, or student ID numbers. Replace them with a student code (S001, S002, ...) first, using the Team Maker Key workbook from the Team Project Field Guide in Module 06. The tool refuses files that look like they contain names or IDs.

The key that links codes to students stays in the workbook on your own computer or IU-approved storage. This tool only ever sees codes and survey answers.

What to ask on the survey
  • Top two regions: "Select your top TWO geographical regions of interest" with First Choice and Second Choice (Africa, Asia, Australia, Europe, North America, South America). Most tools export these as two columns; set both to Shared region (1st/2nd choice).
  • Four 1 to 5 ratings: data analysis confidence, slide deck ability, library research skills, and public speaking confidence (7 slides in 7 minutes). Each is set to Balance a 1 to 5 skill, which also rewards teams with at least one student rating 4 or 5.
  • IU username, so you can swap it for a student code in the Team Maker Key workbook before uploading. Never upload the username itself.
  • Availability: "Which times could you meet outside class?" as a checkbox list (for example Mon AM; Mon PM; Tue AM ...). Most survey tools export these separated by semicolons or commas, which this tool reads.
  • Experience with a skill the project needs, on a 1 to 5 scale (for example, "How comfortable are you writing Python?").
  • Role preference: "On a team, what do you most like to do?" (for example: coding, design, testing, writing, coordinating).
  • Identity (optional, self-described): if you use gender or another identity question, use it only to avoid isolating a student, tell students why you ask, and offer "prefer not to say."

Leave out GPA and anything you would not use. Every extra criterion weakens the others.

Step 2 Choose criteria

For each survey column, choose how it should shape teams and how much it matters (1 is light, 5 is strong). Columns set to Ignore are not used.

Availability

Times are read from every column set to an availability option. Answers can be separated by semicolons, commas, slashes, vertical bars, or line breaks. Day names are matched whether students wrote "Monday" or "Mon," and capitalization and extra spaces are ignored. With more than one availability column (for example, one column per day), each answer is labeled with its column.

Time slots found, with how many students chose each. Uncheck any that should not count as a meeting time (for example "None" or a slot during class).

Teams that cannot divide evenly get one more or one fewer member. Oakley and colleagues recommend teams of three or four.

Step 3 Review, adjust, and export

Teams are listed from weakest fit to strongest, so the ones that need a look come first. Move a student with the menu beside their name; scores update as you go. Run Form teams again for a different arrangement.

Teams appear here after you select Form teams.

The team list has two columns, student_code and group_name, and no names. To import it into Canvas groups, paste it into tab 3 of the Team Maker Key workbook, which matches each code to an IU username on your own computer and builds the Canvas file (login_id, group_name). Then in Canvas, open People, then Groups, create a group set, and choose Import. Download may be unavailable inside some viewers; copy and save the text as a .csv file instead.

Approach adapted from criterion-based team formation research (Layton, Loughry, Ohland, and Ricco, 2010), which scores each team against instructor criteria and improves the worst-fitting team first, and from Oakley, Felder, Brent, and Elhajj (2004).
